# Align your architecture backlog with Tech Roadmap Prioritization (TRP)

## Overview

During digital transformation and cloud migration, architecture teams often face a core challenge: determining priorities among multiple competing initiatives (such as new feature development, application modernization, security hardening). Decision-making without structured processes is often driven by political influence or short-term thinking, causing high-value work to stagnate while low-impact projects consume resources. Tech Roadmap Prioritization (TRP) is a framework designed to solve this problem. Through a guided session of approximately one hour, it brings together business and technical decision-makers to use a visual priority matrix to collectively build an executable architecture backlog roadmap. This is critical for SRE teams because correct prioritization directly impacts the alignment of system reliability investments (such as reducing failure points) with business objectives, and lays the foundation for subsequent capacity planning, project sequencing, and [[error-budget]] management.

## Core Concepts

The core output of TRP is a **visual priority matrix**, adapted from the classic priority matrix and specifically designed for architecture roadmap planning. Each competing initiative is represented as a "bubble," whose position and characteristics are determined by four visual cues:

1.  **X-axis: Cost and Complexity** - From left to right representing low to high. Evaluates the initiative's resource investment, technical risk, and required time.
2.  **Y-axis: Business Impact** - From bottom to top representing low to high. Evaluates the initiative's contribution to business outcomes such as revenue growth, cost savings, risk mitigation, or customer retention.
3.  **Bubble size: Strategic Importance** - The larger the bubble, the more important the initiative's contribution to the organization's strategic goals.
4.  **Bubble color: Strategy Type** - Based on the Modernize, Optimize, Monetize (MOM) framework, reflecting the initiative's primary purpose:
    *   **Yellow (Modernize)**: Improving existing systems (such as application modernization, technical debt repayment).
    *   **Blue (Optimize)**: Reducing costs or improving efficiency (such as cost optimization, performance improvement).
    *   **Green (Monetize)**: Creating new revenue (such as new features, new products).

The bubble's **position** in the matrix directly reveals its priority and the category of action needed:

*   **Upper-left quadrant (High impact, Low cost): Strategic Quick Wins** - Execute immediately. Deliver value quickly and build credibility.
*   **Upper-right quadrant (High impact, High cost): Strategic Transformations** - Require risk reduction first (such as PoC, training, upskilling), then scaled execution.
*   **Lower-left quadrant (Low cost, Low impact): Tactical Quick Wins** - Delegate or batch process, clearing backlog and freeing resources.
*   **Lower-right quadrant (High cost, Low cost): Questionable Initiatives** - Shelve. Keep visible, but do not invest unless business prerequisites change.

The shared priority map produced by TRP can directly feed into the [[cloud-adoption-framework]]'s "Envision" phase, linking business objectives with enabling technologies.

## Practice

### Organizing and Executing the One-Hour Roadmap Session

As the meeting facilitator, your role is to keep discussions focused, ensure process progress, and let all voices be heard, rather than making decisions. Decision-making authority belongs to the attending business and technical leaders.

**1. Pre-meeting preparation**
*   **Invite the right participants**: Must invite people who can make decisions and commit resources, such as CTOs, VPs of Engineering, product managers, and business line leaders. Ensure business and technical decision-makers are in the same room.
*   **Prepare initiative list**: Collect 5-15 competing initiatives before the meeting. Sources can include project proposals, strategic plans, customer requests, and technical debt lists. Write a name and one-sentence description for each initiative to ensure everyone can understand.

**2. During-meeting flow (approximately 60 minutes)**
Guide the team to evaluate each initiative relatively and plot on the matrix around the following four questions:
1.  **How big is it?** (Cost/Complexity) - Skip detailed estimates, only do relative comparisons (e.g., is it a quarter's work or a multi-year plan?).
2.  **How important is it?** (Strategic Importance) - Determine its place in the organization's strategy, who supports it, and why it matters. This determines bubble size.
3.  **How much impact will it have?** (Business Impact) - Clarify the business outcomes it drives (revenue, cost, risk, customers). Place on the Y-axis based on team evaluation.
4.  **Is it Modernize, Optimize, or Monetize?** (Strategy Type) - Assign color and check if the combination is balanced. Be wary of all initiatives being the same type (e.g., all optimization), which may mean other aspects are being neglected.

**Key facilitation tips (Do's & Don'ts)**
*   **DO**: Clarify your role as facilitator at the opening; identify and discuss cross-cutting initiatives that the team sees as "other team's problems"; force relative comparisons when all initiatives are stacked together; note strategy mix imbalance; guide discussions from "what technology is it" back to "what business outcome does it achieve."
*   **DON'T**: Get into technical solution details during the meeting ("how should we do it"); skip pre-meeting preparation, causing meeting time to be spent defining initiatives rather than prioritizing; ignore initiatives that cannot be evaluated for cost or impact, and mark them as needing further exploration.

**3. Post-meeting follow-up**
*   **Assign owners and next steps**: Assign owners and specific dates to the highest-priority initiatives. Ensure every initiative on the matrix has a clear owner and next step (even if it's "re-evaluate in Q3").
*   **Treat the matrix as a living document**: Conduct a formal review at least annually. Additionally, ad-hoc TRP meetings should be held when significant strategic shifts occur, initiatives fail or stagnate, major budget changes happen, new high-priority initiatives emerge, or completed initiatives free up resources.

## SRE Key Points

*   **Relationship with reliability and observability**: The priority map produced by TRP is a key input for SRE teams planning reliability investments (such as improving monitoring, optimizing deployment pipelines, repaying technical debt that increases failure risk). It ensures engineering resources are prioritized for areas with the greatest business impact and assessed risk. "Strategic Transformation" initiatives in the upper-right corner of the matrix (such as large-scale migrations) especially require early SRE involvement to define measurable reliability goals (SLIs/SLOs) and rollback strategies during migration.
*   **Best practices and common anti-patterns**
    *   **Best practices**: Use TRP as the starting point for annual technology roadmaps; combine the matrix with structured analysis tools like [[cloud-adoption-framework]]; use the matrix as a common language for communicating with executives, delivery teams, and partners, documenting decision rationale.
    *   **Common anti-patterns**: Conducting technical debates or solution design during TRP meetings; holding meetings without preparation; ignoring initiatives that cannot be evaluated, leaving ambiguity; shelving the produced matrix after the meeting, failing to regularly review and update it.
*   **Related metrics (SLI/SLO/SLA)**
    *   The TRP matrix itself is a **strategic-level** decision tool. It does not directly produce SLIs/SLOs but provides context for defining them. For example, for an optimization initiative marked as "Strategic Quick Win" (such as improving API performance), the SRE team should immediately work with the product owner to define specific availability or latency SLOs.
    *   The **dynamic updating** of the matrix is an indirect indicator of TRP process effectiveness. A healthy technology roadmap should show initiatives moving between quadrants (e.g., a "Strategic Transformation" project may see its cost/complexity assessment decrease after PoC validation).
    *   Systems identified through TRP as needing priority attention should have their corresponding **SLA compliance** and **Error Budget consumption rate** become the focus of subsequent SRE monitoring and reporting.
