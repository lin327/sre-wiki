## Overview

This article is based on the Prometheus official blog's interview with Hostinger, a large hosting service provider, and provides an in-depth analysis of the modernization and evolution of its monitoring system. As an international service provider with more than 30 million customers, Hostinger migrated from the traditional Nagios/Cacti/Ganglia (NCG) stack to the Prometheus ecosystem, and based on this, solved the challenges of performance, high availability, and user experience under large-scale monitoring. This case provides the SRE team with valuable practical experience in monitoring system selection, architecture evolution, performance optimization, and building an observability system.

## Core concepts

In the case of Hostinger, several key monitoring and SRE concepts are involved:

1. **Monitoring stack modernization**: Migrate from the NCG stack (Nagios, Cacti, Ganglia) based on threshold checking and simple drawing to the Prometheus stack based on powerful data models, rich ecology and native support for multi-dimensional data.
2. **Time Series Database (TSDB)**: As the core storage engine, Prometheus’s writing performance, query capabilities and storage efficiency are the basis for supporting large-scale monitoring.
3. **High Availability (HA) Architecture**: By deploying redundant components (such as two Prometheus nodes, two Alertmanagers) and using load balancers (such as HAProxy), ensure that the monitoring system itself can continue to work in the event of a single point of failure or regional outage.
4. **Cache Layer**: Introducing a caching proxy like Trickster, designed to speed up dashboard queries, reduce duplicate requests to backend Prometheus, and prevent "thunder herd problems" that may occur during failover.

## Practice

Hostinger considers monitoring to be a core pillar of its business reliability and has implemented the following specific measures in practice:

* **Progressive Migration and Technology Assessment**: Prometheus was introduced when the automation project was launched in 2015, and went through a transition period of running the new and old systems in parallel, and finally switched completely. The TICK (Telegraf/InfluxDB/Chronograf/Kapacitor) technology stack was evaluated before the migration, but due to its functional limitations and maturity, the simpler and more mature Prometheus was chosen.
* **Make full use of the Exporter ecosystem**: Deployed about 25 community-developed indicator exporters, and wrote custom exporters (such as `lxc_exporter`). For network devices, run `node_exporter` directly on supported "white box" switches (x86 architecture running CumulusOS) as an alternative to traditional SNMP, exposing more human-readable metrics.
* **Business indicator integration**: Through the `textfile collector` mechanism of Prometheus, customized business-related indicators (such as indicators related to customer billing or package usage) are exposed to the monitoring system, achieving unified monitoring of technical indicators and business indicators.
* **Solve the bottlenecks and high availability of the monitoring system itself**:
    * **Performance and Storage**: As the infrastructure scales up to about 2,000 servers, Prometheus itself becomes a bottleneck, with disk usage reaching about 2TB and restarting taking 10-15 minutes. Plan to upgrade to Prometheus 2.6 to take advantage of performance improvements and features such as WAL (write-ahead logs).
    * **High-availability deployment**: Implemented a two-node high-availability architecture across continents, including two independent Prometheus instances and two Alertmanager instances that form a cluster. The Alertmanager cluster can deduplicate identical alarms.
    * **Query load balancing and caching**: Deploy HAProxy and Trickster between Grafana and Prometheus. HAProxy provides local access and failover; Trickster caches time series data in memory, which greatly improves dashboard loading speed and avoids impact on backup nodes when the primary node fails.
* **Future expansion plans**: Plans to deploy a large number of `blackbox_exporter` to monitor website external availability for all Hostinger customers. In the long term, consider sharding Prometheus to further scale and isolate fault domains.

## SRE Key Points

Hostinger's practices embody SRE's core principles of focusing on reliability, scalability, and efficiency.

* **Correlation with Reliability**: The high availability (HA) deployment of a monitoring system directly improves its own reliability as a critical infrastructure. Reducing the mean time to detect failures (MTTD) from 5 minutes to seconds (MTTD reduced by 4 times) is the key to improving the reliability of the entire hosting platform. Using `blackbox_exporter` to actively detect external services embodies the SRE philosophy of "anything that cannot be monitored cannot be evaluated".
* **Best Practices and Anti-Patterns**:
    * **Best Practices**: Monitor everything (technical and business indicators); protect the back-end system and improve user experience through the caching layer (Trickster); design a high-availability architecture for the monitoring system itself; use standardized Exporters to avoid reinventing the wheel.
    * **Anti-Pattern Warning**: A single point of failure caused by a single monitoring instance; failure to cache and limit the flow of dashboard queries may lead to back-end overload (thunder group problem); neglect of the monitoring system's own capacity planning and performance optimization (such as restart time).
* **Related indicators (SLI/SLO/SLA)**:
    * **SLI (Service Level Indicator)**: Time resolution (from 5 minutes to 15 seconds), Mean Time to Detection (MTTD), Dashboard load time.
    * **SLO (Service Level Objective)**: monitoring system availability target (such as 99.9%), timeliness and accuracy of alarms.
    * **SLA (Service Level Agreement)**: Although not mentioned directly, the reliability of the monitoring system ultimately underpins the service availability SLA promised to the customer.

Through this case, SRE can learn how to build an open source monitoring tool from a simple alarm system into an observability platform that supports a large business, has flexibility and self-protection capabilities.
