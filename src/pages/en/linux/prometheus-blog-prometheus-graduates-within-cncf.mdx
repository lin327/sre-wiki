# Prometheus Blog - Prometheus Graduates Within CNCF

## Overview

Prometheus officially graduated from CNCF (Cloud Native Computing Foundation) in August 2018, becoming the second project after Kubernetes to achieve this highest level. This milestone is not only a major achievement for the Prometheus project itself, but also sets a benchmark for observability tools across the cloud native ecosystem. In the practice of Site Reliability Engineering (SRE), choosing a mature, stable and well-governed monitoring system is the basis for building an observability system. The graduation of Prometheus provides the SRE team with strong quality endorsement and decision-making basis when selecting internal tools.

## Core concepts

**CNCF Graduation Level**: CNCF projects are divided into three maturity levels: Sandbox, Incubation and Graduated. Project graduation means that CNCF has full confidence in its code quality, feature iteration speed, project maturity and stability, and governance and community processes. This represents that the project has reached high standards of production readiness in terms of technology, operations and community governance.

**Prometheus’ maturing evolution**: Graduation doesn’t happen overnight. As it moves from incubation to graduation, Prometheus has completed a number of key efforts, including a complete rewrite of the storage backend to support highly dynamically changing services, major improvements to system stability (especially in version 2.3.2), and advancement of documentation focused on simplifying adoption and contribution. Together, these efforts form the technical foundation for achieving graduation standards.

## Practice

The graduation of Prometheus reflects its maturity in design and community support as a monitoring system. For SRE teams, this means more confidence in using Prometheus as the core monitoring stack.

**Evolution of technical architecture**: Before graduation, Prometheus completely rewritten its storage backend. This improvement directly enhances the system's ability to process high churn indicators, enabling it to better adapt to scenarios where service instances change frequently in modern microservice architectures, ensuring the continuity and reliability of monitoring data.

**Universality of Adoption Stages**: The popularity of Prometheus has gone through four clear stages, which also illustrates the universality of its design:
1. **Early Adopters**: First adopted by users who are focused on finding top monitoring tools.
2. **Ultra-large-scale users**: Users who face existing monitoring solutions that cannot meet their scale turn to Prometheus.
3. **Infrastructure Reinventor**: From small businesses to Fortune 500 companies, choose this when rebuilding their surveillance systems.
4. **Users with limited resources**: The majority of users who lack dedicated funds and resources to invest in monitoring, but start to adopt it after hearing about its benefits.

For SRE teams, understanding this adoption path can help evaluate where your team is in adopting Prometheus and what challenges and benefits it may face.

## SRE Key Points

* **Reliability and Quality Assurance**: Prometheus’ CNCF graduation is a strong signal that its **reliability** is widely recognized. For SREs, relying on a tool with a complete governance process that has been verified by an external authority (such as CNCF) can significantly reduce supply chain risks and ensure the stability of the monitoring system itself. This is directly related to the accuracy of [[slo]] and [[error-budget]] calculations.
* **Community and Governance**: Graduation proves the success of its open and transparent community governance model. The SRE culture emphasizes learning and collaboration, and a healthy community means rich knowledge sharing, rapid bug fixes, and continuous feature innovation, which are critical to long-term maintenance of the monitoring stack.
* **Best Practice**: When evaluating any critical infrastructure tool, consider Prometheus' Graduation Criteria for: **code quality and test coverage**, **release process and backward compatibility commitment**, **documentation completeness and ease of use**, and **community activity and contributor governance model**.
* **Related Indicators (SLI/SLO)**: Although graduation itself is not SLI, it indirectly guarantees the reliability of the monitoring data source. SRE teams can define "availability and data integrity of core monitoring systems (such as Prometheus)" as an SLI and set SLOs based on their health to ensure that the observability platform itself meets reliability requirements.
