# Interview with ForgeRock

## Overview

ForgeRock is an international software company providing identity and access management (IAM) solutions, with a platform comprised of multiple core products. Before migrating to a cloud-native architecture (Docker and Kubernetes), ForgeRock faced the challenge of monitoring fragmentation: each product provided different monitoring interfaces (such as SNMP, JMX, LDAP, REST API), making overall monitoring complex and requiring multiple sets of tools. To solve this problem, ForgeRock chose Prometheus as a unified monitoring foundation, which not only simplifies quality engineering and problem diagnosis within the platform, but also provides its customers with a standardized and powerful observability solution.

## Core concepts

### Data model adaptation: dimension vs. hierarchy

A key technical challenge ForgeRock encountered during the migration process was differences in monitoring data models. Prometheus and its ecosystem (such as Micrometer) tend to use the Dimensions model, which uses labels to distinguish different dimensions of the same indicator (such as `method="GET"`, `status="200"`). The original indicator system within ForgeRock is more accustomed to the **hierarchical** structure, that is, nesting information through the indicator name itself (such as `requests.get.success`).

To be compatible with both models, ForgeRock did not choose to compromise, but built a custom adaptation layer. They encapsulated it based on the Micrometer interface and adjusted the DropWizard Prometheus Exporter. This allows their single instrumentation code to simultaneously expose metrics as dimensions or hierarchies, thus smoothing the transition to Prometheus while retaining backwards compatibility.

### Hierarchical monitoring architecture

In the process of evolving to the "Identity as a Service" (IDaaS) model, ForgeRock built a clear two-level Prometheus monitoring architecture:

1. **Tenant-level monitoring**: Deploy an independent Prometheus instance for each customer (tenant) environment, collect detailed indicators of the environment, and provide customers with self-monitoring and customized Grafana dashboards.
2. **Central Service Monitoring**: Build a centralized Prometheus service to aggregate key indicators of all tenant environments through Remote Write or similar mechanisms. This gives ForgeRock’s SRE team a holistic view of the overall health and operating patterns of all customer environments.

This architecture not only meets the needs of customers for autonomy, but also ensures the global observability and operation and maintenance efficiency of the platform provider.

## Practice

### Transition path

ForgeRock’s migration did not happen overnight, but took pragmatic and incremental steps:

1. **Unified instrumentation library**: Select DropWizard as the basic indicator collection library and apply it uniformly to all products.
2. **Adaptation and Integration**: Solve the conflict between Prometheus's dimensional model and its own hierarchical model, and achieve flexible exposure by building a custom adaptation layer (based on the Micrometer interface and the modified Exporter).
3. **Provide monitoring out of the box**: Develop a series of sample Grafana dashboards and provide them with the product to help them quickly start monitoring and allow them to customize them.
4. **Maintain compatibility**: Although the use of Prometheus is highly recommended, ForgeRock still retains the old SNMP, JMX and other monitoring interfaces to ensure a smooth transition.

### Monitoring-driven quality improvement

After switching to Prometheus, a significant improvement occurred in the quality engineering (QE) team's workflow:

* **Monitoring enabled by default**: The QE team enables Prometheus metric collection by default during stress testing and performance testing.
* **Customized test dashboard**: They customize the Grafana dashboard for specific performance testing scenarios to make the test results visual and quantifiable.
* **Locate performance bottlenecks**: By analyzing monitoring indicators, the QE team can more accurately point out performance problems to the engineering team, greatly improving the efficiency of problem diagnosis.
* **Engineering team adoption**: The engineering team also began to rely on Prometheus data when reproducing and fixing problems, and further expanded the relevant dashboards. This process forms a positive cycle: **Testing finds problems -&gt; Monitoring provides insights -&gt; Engineering fixes and verifies**, ultimately improving product quality.

## SRE Key Points

### Relationship with reliability and observability

ForgeRock's practice is an example of using monitoring to improve **Reliability**. A unified monitoring dashboard provides their SRE/QA teams with clear Observability, allowing them to proactively identify performance degradation, verify fixes, and gain a deep understanding of how the system behaves under load. This data-based decision-making culture directly reduces "guess-making" troubleshooting and improves mean time to repair (MTTR).

### Best practices and common anti-patterns

* **Best Practice**:
    * **Unified Monitoring Interface**: Provides a consistent monitoring experience for complex platforms, reducing usage costs for internal and external users.
    * **Progressive Migration**: Maintain backward compatibility, encourage but not force users to migrate, and reduce migration resistance.
    * **Monitor-driven development**: Integrate the construction of monitoring capabilities into the development and testing process, making "observability" a part of the product from the beginning.
    * **Layering and aggregation**: Design a multi-level monitoring architecture to balance the needs of customer autonomy and centralized platform operation and maintenance.
* **Common anti-patterns (ForgeRock circumvents these)**:
    * **Technical lock-in**: Force customers to use a specific monitoring backend.
    * **Monitoring Island**: Different products or teams use completely separated monitoring systems, unable to form a global view.
    * **After-the-fact Remedy**: Monitoring is considered only in the later stages of product development, resulting in unreasonable or missing indicator design.

### Related Indicators (SLI/SLO/SLA)

While specific SLIs/SLOs were not explicitly mentioned in the interviews, ForgeRock’s practices provide a solid data foundation for defining SLOs for the identity platform. For example, they can easily obtain and define the following SLI from Prometheus:

* **Identity authentication request success rate**: `sum(rate(auth_requests_total{status=~"2.."}[5m])) / sum(rate(auth_requests_total[5m]))`
* **Identity token issuance delay**: `histogram_quantile(0.99, rate(token_issuance_duration_seconds_bucket[5m]))`
* **Directory Service Query Availability**: Based on the success rate of the health check endpoint.

These SLIs can be further tied to the **SLO** and **SLA** signed by the customer, thereby converting technical indicators into commercial commitments and guiding the consumption of the Error Budget.
