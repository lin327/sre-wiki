## Overview

When maintaining a Kubernetes-based production environment, etcd version upgrade is a critical and cautious task. This document focuses on a specific risk that may be encountered when upgrading from etcd v3.5 to v3.6: the creation of "zombie cluster members". These are nodes that have been removed but then reappear and join consensus, causing the entire etcd cluster to become unavailable. Understanding the root cause of this problem and following the correct upgrade path are key SRE practices to ensure etcd reliability and avoid major incidents.

## Core concepts

### Zombie Members
Zombie members refer to those nodes that have been marked as removed in the etcd cluster metadata and no longer participate in the consensus, but due to data inconsistency issues, unexpectedly rejoin the cluster after an upgrade or restart and try to participate in the Raft consensus. This disrupts the cluster's election and log replication processes, ultimately causing the entire cluster to be out of service.

### Storage inconsistency: v2store and v3store
The root of the problem lies in the two storage locations of etcd cluster membership information:
- **v2store**: The source of truth for member information in etcd v3.5 and earlier versions.
- **v3store**: Starting from etcd v3.6, the true source of member information.

Under certain historical operations or failure scenarios, the member lists in the two stores may become inconsistent. When upgrading to v3.6 (subject to v3store), the old member information remaining in v2store will cause zombie members to be "resurrected".

### Safe upgrade path
To solve this problem, the etcd community introduced a fix mechanism in v3.5.26. The only officially recommended safe upgrade path is to first upgrade to v3.5.26 or higher. This version automatically detects and fixes inconsistencies between v2store and v3store, ensuring that no zombie members will appear during subsequent upgrades to v3.6.

## Practice

### Security upgrade steps
To ensure a safe upgrade of the etcd cluster from v3.5 to v3.6, the following steps must be strictly followed:

1. **Step 1: Upgrade to v3.5.26**
    Upgrade all etcd nodes in the cluster to `v3.5.26` or higher (such as v3.5.27). This is a crucial step in the entire upgrade process. Its purpose is to trigger the automatic repair mechanism and synchronize the member data of v3store and v2store.

2. **Step 2: Wait and confirm cluster health**
    After all nodes have completed the v3.5.26 upgrade, you must wait and verify that the cluster is fully healthy. Use tools such as `etcdctl endpoint health` to check the status of each member to ensure that they are all `healthy` and `leader` elections are normal. This waiting process is for the repair mechanism to fully take effect.

3. **Step 3: Upgrade to v3.6**
    After confirming that the cluster is healthy, you can start upgrading the cluster to the target version v3.6.x. At this time, the upgrade process will be safe as member data has been fixed in the v3.5.26 phase.

**IMPORTANT**: If v3.5.26 is temporarily unavailable due to package or vendor reasons, **the upgrade to v3.6 should be postponed** until v3.5.26 or higher is available. There is no known safe way to bypass this path.

### Known problem trigger scenarios (for reference)
While problems can be avoided by following safe paths, understanding the source of the trigger can help troubleshoot risks:
- **Old version of `etcdctl snapshot restore` tool defect (v3.4)**: Old members were not removed correctly when restoring the snapshot, resulting in residual member information.
- **Use `--force-new-cluster` flag (before v3.5.22)**: When forcing the creation of a single-member cluster, old member information may not be completely cleaned up. This issue has been fixed in v3.5.22.
- **Enable `--unsafe-no-sync` flag**: This may cause member changes to be written only to v3store but not to the write-ahead log, causing data inconsistency after a crash. **This flag is generally deprecated**.

## SRE Key Points

- **Relationship with Reliability**: etcd is the core database of the Kubernetes control plane, and its availability directly determines the reliability of the entire cluster. The zombie member problem caused by the upgrade operation is a typical "Configuration Change Induced Failure", which SRE must prevent through process control. Strict, phased upgrade operations are best practices in change management.
- **Relevance to Observability**: A detailed health check of the etcd cluster must be performed before and after performing upgrade operations. This is not only checking the health status of `etcdctl`, but also monitoring the cluster's latency, leader election frequency, disk I/O and other SLI. Any anomalous indicators that appear after an upgrade should trigger an investigation to confirm whether such data inconsistencies were encountered.
- **Best Practices and Common Anti-Patterns**:
    - **BEST PRACTICE**: Always read and follow official, version-specific upgrade guides. Perform upgrades within a planning window and roll them out to production in stages (e.g., verify on a pre-release environment first). The verification steps included in the upgrade path (such as waiting for health) are as important as the upgrade steps themselves.
    - **Common anti-patterns**: Jumping directly from a lower version to a higher version; ignoring necessary transition versions (such as v3.5.26); introducing other untested configuration changes during the upgrade process.
- **Related Metrics (SLI/SLO/SLA)**: Clear SLOs should be defined for etcd services, for example: `API request latency for p99 should be less than 50ms`, `Cluster availability should exceed 99.9%`. Any changes that violate this SLO, including version upgrades, should be considered a high-risk operation and require a detailed rollback plan. The success of the upgrade operation should ultimately be measured by whether it does not degrade or continues to meet the SLO.

**Summary**: For major version upgrades of etcd, the core responsibility of SRE is to **avoid risks**. This upgrade path from etcd v3.5 to v3.6 clearly shows that skipping the critical fixative intermediate version (v3.5.26) will directly lead to production failures. Therefore, establishing and strictly executing an upgrade manual that includes version dependency checks is the cornerstone of ensuring the reliability of basic components.
