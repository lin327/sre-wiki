# Grafana Blog - Eliminate noisy log lines with Adaptive Logs drop rules

## Overview

In modern cloud-native architecture and observability practice, log data is a valuable resource for diagnosing problems and understanding system behavior. However, massive log streams are often interspersed with large amounts of low-value, high-frequency "noise," such as frequent health check logs, verbose debug logs, or redundant information from non-critical services. This noise not only rapidly consumes valuable log storage budgets but also increases query and analysis complexity, distracting operations teams from locating real issues.

Grafana Cloud's **Adaptive Logs** feature provides an intelligent log management solution. Its latest **Drop Rules** capability allows platform and observability teams to proactively discard or sample known noise data before logs are written to storage, based on custom rules. This provides teams with a fast, centralized means to optimize log ingestion volume, reduce costs, and ensure critical logs are fully preserved, without requiring cumbersome infrastructure changes.

## Core Concepts

Adaptive Logs manages incoming log lines through an ordered evaluation pipeline, with three mechanisms working in sequence to process logs:

1.  **Exemptions**: Evaluated first. Any log line matching exemption rules is fully protected and passes through directly, **without being sampled or discarded by any means**. This ensures the integrity and auditability of business-critical logs (such as audit logs, payment error logs).
2.  **Drop Rules**: Evaluated after exemption assessment, in priority order. Log lines are matched against user-defined drop rules, **the first matching rule takes effect**, and applies its defined discard rate (which can be 100% complete discard or partial sampling).
3.  **Pattern Optimization Suggestions (Patterns)**: Finally, for log lines not exempted or filtered by drop rules, Adaptive Logs analyzes their query patterns. The system can suggest optimizations for unused log patterns (such as reducing sampling rates or discarding) based on analysis results.

These three mechanisms together form a complete log cost control system, addressing log noise from different dimensions:
- **Drop Rules** target noise sources the team **already knows about**.
- **Optimization Suggestions** help discover waste the team **hasn't yet noticed**.
- **Exemption Rules** protect **non-negotiable** critical logs.

## Practices

### Configuring Drop Rules

The interface for creating and managing drop rules is located in Grafana Cloud's admin console. Specific steps:

1.  **Navigate to Admin Interface**: Enter the Grafana Cloud console, click **Adaptive Telemetry** -> **Adaptive Logs** -> **Drop rules** tab in sequence.
2.  **Create New Rule**: Click the **Create a new drop rule** button.
3.  **Define Rule Conditions**: Use a combination of **Label Selectors**, **Log Levels**, and **Line Content Matching** to define the rule's scope.
    -   **Drop by Level**: For example, create a rule to discard all `level:debug` logs.
    -   **Sample Repeated Logs**: Set a discard percentage (e.g., 90%) for specific streams, retaining only representative samples.
    -   **Target Specific Producers**: Through labels (e.g., `service="checkout-api"`) combined with log level or text strings, precisely filter logs from a noisy service.
4.  **Immediate Effect and Monitoring**: Rules take effect immediately after saving. You can monitor the volume of logs discarded by each rule on the **Drop rules** page to assess its impact.

### Management and Permissions

-   **Permission Control**: Creating and managing drop rules requires the **Adaptive Logs admin role** (`plugins:grafana-adaptivelogs-app:admin`). This role is granted by default to Grafana administrators and organization administrators, ensuring rule management is centrally controlled by the team responsible for observability costs and policies.
-   **CLI Support**: In addition to the Web UI, you can also use Grafana's `gcx` command-line tool to create and manage drop rules, facilitating automation integration.
-   **Emergency Override**: In emergency situations (such as troubleshooting, important deployments), you can **pause Adaptive Logs** for up to one hour, temporarily overriding all drop rules to obtain complete log visibility. Combined with persistent exemption rules, this provides necessary flexibility.

## SRE Key Points

### Connection to Reliability, Observability, and Incident Response

Drop rules directly serve core SRE goals: achieving balance between cost and reliability. By eliminating useless logs, teams can more efficiently query and analyze truly important data, **improving the efficiency and accuracy of fault localization**. When incidents occur, the ability to quickly trace back critical logs is key to MTTR (Mean Time to Repair). Adaptive Logs' exemption and pause mechanisms ensure that **complete observability** of critical logs is not lost during incident response.

### Best Practices and Common Anti-Patterns

**Best Practices**:
-   **Gradual Approach**: Start with simple rules, such as "discard 90% of DEBUG-level logs from a specific noisy service." Observe the effect before developing more complex strategies.
-   **Centralized Management**: Utilize its admin role feature, with SRE or platform teams centrally formulating and managing discard policies rather than dispersing to individual business teams, ensuring policy consistency and controllability.
-   **Combined Usage**: Use drop rules together with system-recommended pattern optimization suggestions. Drop rules handle known issues; optimization suggestions uncover unknown waste.
-   **Explicit Exemptions**: For compliance audits, core business errors, and similar logs, be sure to set **exemption rules** — this is the bottom line for cost optimization.

**Common Anti-Patterns**:
-   **Over-Discarding**: Blindly discarding large volumes of logs to avoid costs may discard information useful for future root cause analysis. Decisions should be based on data and business impact.
-   **Ignoring Monitoring**: Setting rules and never reviewing their impact data. Continuously monitor each rule's discard volume to ensure it meets expectations and hasn't accidentally discarded critical data.
-   **Bypassing Processes**: Due to this feature's convenience, individual teams may attempt to create their own rules to "solve" their service's log problems. Centralized management through the platform team should be insisted upon to maintain the overall observability strategy.

### Related Metrics

While drop rules themselves do not directly define SLI/SLO, they affect operational metrics such as cost and query performance:
-   **Log Ingestion Volume (GB/day)**: This is the metric most directly reduced by drop rules, directly tied to storage costs.
-   **Log Query Latency/Performance**: After data volume reduction, log query speed typically improves, which can indirectly optimize developer experience and troubleshooting efficiency.
-   **Critical Log Integrity**: Ensuring that business-critical logs protected by exemption rules (such as payment failure logs) maintain a 100% sampling rate can be monitored as an **SLI** for compliance and reliability.
